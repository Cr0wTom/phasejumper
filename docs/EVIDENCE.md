# The research behind your plan

PhaseJumper turns published sleep and body-clock research into a day-by-day plan. This page explains, in plain language, what each part of the plan is based on and how strong the evidence is. It is general wellness information, not medical advice.

Evidence grades are our own summary: **A** means repeated human studies or controlled trials, **B** means at least one controlled human study, **C** means plausible but indirect.

## Your body clock and the plan

Your body runs on an internal clock of about 24 hours. Its daily low point in core body temperature, usually a couple of hours before you wake, is the marker the plan steers by. After a flight across time zones, that clock is out of step with local time until it catches up. The plan works out which way to move it (earlier or later), how far, and how many days it should take, then schedules each day around where your clock is expected to be.

- How to plan a trip's sleep and light day by day — Eastman and Burgess, 2009. [Read the article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2829880/)
- Two processes that shape sleep: sleep pressure that builds while awake, and the body clock — Borbély and colleagues, 2016. [Find on PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=The+two-process+model+of+sleep+regulation%3A+a+reappraisal)

## Light: the strongest signal (grade A)

Bright light is what moves the body clock most. Light in the hours after your clock's low point moves it earlier; light in the hours before moves it later. That is why the plan asks you to seek light at some times and keep things dim at others. Short sessions of bright light work too, so the plan uses windows of about an hour.

- How the timing of bright light shifts the body clock — Khalsa and colleagues, 2003. [Find on PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=A+phase+response+curve+to+single+bright+light+pulses+in+human+subjects)
- One hour of bright light has the same pattern of effect — St Hilaire and colleagues, 2012. [Find on PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=Human+phase+response+curve+to+a+1+h+pulse+of+bright+white+light)

## Moving your sleep with your clock (grade A)

Rather than switching to local time overnight, the plan moves your sleep window a little each day, in step with your clock. Starting before you fly can help on eastward trips.

- Shifting sleep and light before an eastward flight — Eastman and colleagues, 2005. [Read the article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1262683/)

## Melatonin timing, only if you already use it (grade A)

If you tell the planner you already use melatonin, it shows when research suggests taking it can help move your clock. When the plan moves your clock earlier, it shows a range from the afternoon until bedtime when melatonin can help, and a best time inside it, about 5 to 6 hours before your new bedtime. You can choose a later best time, about 4 to 5 hours before bedtime, which research suggests still works well and leaves less of your day drowsy. At bedtime its effect on the clock is small. It never suggests whether to use melatonin, which product, or how much. In some countries melatonin is sold only with a doctor's order. Its timing is not shown to anyone who is pregnant, takes other medication, or is under 18. Follow the advice of whoever provided it.

- How the timing of melatonin shifts the body clock — Burgess and colleagues, 2008 and 2010. [Find on PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=A+three+pulse+phase+response+curve+to+three+milligrams+of+melatonin+in+humans)
- When melatonin moves the body clock earlier and when it moves it later — Lewy, 2010. [Read the article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2928905/)

## Exercise: a smaller nudge (grade B)

Exercise at certain times of day can move the body clock a little, much less than light. If you turn exercise on, the plan suggests windows that help and marks times to avoid, including the hours just before sleep. The way we line up the study's clock times with your clock is our own reading of the research.

- How the timing of exercise shifts the body clock — Youngstedt and colleagues, 2019. [Read the article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6462487/)

## Caffeine and naps: for alertness (grade B)

Caffeine helps you stay alert, but late in the day it can delay your clock and disturb sleep, so the plan shows when your last caffeine should be. Short naps help on days when your night is cut short.

- Late caffeine and the body clock — Burke and colleagues, 2015. [Find on PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=Effects+of+caffeine+on+the+human+circadian+clock+in+vivo+and+in+vitro)
- Caffeine taken hours before bed still affects sleep — Drake and colleagues, 2013. [Find on PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=Caffeine+effects+on+sleep+taken+0%2C+3%2C+or+6+hours+before+going+to+bed)

## Meals and winding down (grade C)

Meal times and a short wind-down before sleep are comfort layers. They do not move the body clock in any well-proven way, so the plan keeps them gentle and never lets them conflict with sleep.

## What the plan cannot know

The plan estimates your body clock from your usual sleep times; it cannot measure it. Real trips have delays, events and bad nights. When something gets in the way of a light window, the plan expects a smaller shift that day and adds time rather than pretending.
